Coordinator session

A session can drive others: it creates child sessions, each in its own folder, writes to them, reads their replies and moves them along, while you follow everything in the sidebar.

A coordinator and its three child sessions.

Make a session a coordinator

Turn on the Coordinate chip in the composer, the one of an open conversation or of a new session. It is offered for Claude Code and Codex, on their service or on an endpoint. Turned on while the agent is running, it restarts nothing: your next message tells it that it can now coordinate. An agent started before version 1.1 lacks the tools it needs; Restart to Coordinate starts it again with them.

Then ask for what you want in plain words: “Take the three tickets of milestone 4, one worktree and one session per ticket, and tell me when they are ready for review.”

Child sessions

A child session is a session like any other: its agent, its folder or worktree, its terminal, its notes, its status. You can open it, write to it and answer its requests yourself. The coordinator can:

  • create a child session, with its name, folder, agent and first prompt;
  • send it a message, read its conversation and wait for the end of its turn;
  • change its status, and close or restart it.

A coordinator never answers a child's permissions and questions for you: they wait for you in the Pending Requests palette, like those of any session.

Coordination has only one level: a child session does not coordinate in turn. What a coordinator did in a child shows in that child's history, under “Coordinated by…”.

In the sidebar

Children show under their coordinator, indented, linked to it by a thin line. Collapse the coordinator to hide its children. When a search or a filter singles out a child, it shows on its own, without the line.

Settings

Settings › Coordination › Children running at once limits how many agents a coordinator runs at the same time: each takes memory and tokens. An automatic archive rule can target coordinators or children with coordination.
